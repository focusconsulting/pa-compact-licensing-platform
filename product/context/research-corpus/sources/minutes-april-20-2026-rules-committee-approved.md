## Draft Rule 7 - State Compliance

- Chair Loucka opens discussion on Draft Rule 7 - State Compliance.
- Section 7.2 Requirements for Member States Implementing Criminal Background Checks
- o A. Houge - Should the rule account for variance in criminal background checks between states?
- § L. Marx asks how the criminal background checks differ.
- § A. Houge specifies that to get a license in Iowa, fingerprinting was not required, whereas California required an FBI background check with fingerprinting.
- § N. Kalfas notes that for purposes of entry into the compact portal, states like Iowa must require a biometric-based criminal background check as set out and required in the compact.
- § J. Alley shares in the meeting chat the compact definition of criminal background check: ''Criminal Background Check' means the submission of fingerprints or other biometric based information for a License
- Larry Marx seconds.
- All members present voted in favor; none abstained; motion passed.

- applicant for the purpose of obtaining that applicant's criminal history record information, as defined in 28 C.F.R. § 20.3(d), from the State's criminal history record repository as defined in 28 C.F.R. § 20.3(f).'
- § S. Gile asks if it is possible that states receive different information from their background checks, such as minor convictions in addition to adult convictions and arrests.
- § N. Kalfas notes that due to some states having Clean Slate Laws, which stipulate that states will not share criminal history information after a certain amount of time has passed and certain stipulations are met, many boards cannot view licensees' minor convictions as part of background checks. While the compact reads as a complete ban on felony and misdemeanor convictions, the Commission needs to determine what is expected of state boards in terms of compact eligibility determinations if certain licensee criminal history information is not available/viewable by those boards. Eventually, this committee may determine each member state must receive the same information from their criminal background checks. Compact law should override conflicting state laws on this point. It is not necessary to make determinations on these points for the purpose of moving this rule forward.
- § S. Gile notes in Kansas only adult convictions and arrests turn up on background checks.
- § N. Kalfas asks if the Kansas licensure application asks about minor convictions and felonies.
- § S. Gile confirms the application includes questions regarding prior convictions, but the board may not receive information to verify attestations separately.
- o Chair Loucka asks if the committee wishes to add an attestation requirement to the rule.
- § J. Alley suggests making it part of the privilege application process through the compact data system.
- § L. Monick notes Rule 3 - Compact Privilege requires that a PA submit an online application to the Commission's data system along with a sworn attestation that all information in the privilege application is true. The application and attestation could include a question about the applicant's criminal history.
- § Committee considers the application and attestation requirements in Rule 3 to be sufficient.
- Section 7.3 Dispute Resolution Process - Informal, Mediation, and Arbitration
- o Chair Loucka notes that section numbers 3.3 and 3.4 in the draft should be changed to 7.3 and 7.4, respectively, as a formatting, non-substantive change. This numbering is left over from the previous rule that this rule was based on.
- § No objections to this change.
- o Chair Loucka notes the rule is intended to encourage states to settle disputes amongst themselves, while also setting out processes for formal resolution.

- o L. Monick notes that compact law states the committee shall promulgate a rule providing for both mediation and finding dispute resolution for disputes as appropriate, and this rule arose out of that requirement.
- o S. Gile and J. Alley note the rule is well-written, and it will likely require putting the process in place to determine whether adjustments are needed.
- Section 7.4 Compliance and Enforcement
- o No comments from the committee.
- Chair Loucka requests a motion to adopt Draft Rule 7 - State Compliance as amended and transmit to the Executive Committee.

## Draft Bylaw Amendments

- Chair Loucka opens discussion on proposed amendments to the Commission Bylaws.
- o L. Monick explains that the amendments address commission vacancies and provide other cleanup in several sections:
- § Article 2 paragraphs are now numbered to align with formatting in other articles.
- § Article 2, section 2 adds language surrounding the notice period required if a temporary representative attends a meeting on behalf of a delegate.
- § Article 3 is updated to clarify that chair, vice chair, secretary, and treasurer are officers of the Commission. Previously, they were identified within the Executive Committee section.
- § Article 3, section 6 now allows for Executive Committee vacancies to be temporarily appointed until an election can be held, except that vice chair would rise to chair if a vacancy occurs in that position.
- § Article 5 strikes the title 'Emergency Meetings' that was out of place.
- § Article 6, section 1, C.1 removed phrase 'as a delegate' as the article discusses committee members.
- § Article 6, section 1, D.2.c added that the Compliance Committee may provide technical assistance and training, which aligns with Draft Rule 7 State Compliance.
- § Article 7, section 1 updates state annual assessment language so that it can be established by rule to align with other draft rules.
- o J. Alley asks if 'as a delegate' should be reinstated in Article 6, section 1, C.1. 'Delegate' was specified during drafting because ex-officios serve on committees, and their appointment does not fall within the province of the bylaws. 'Delegate' is defined as a state representative, and the phrase 'as a delegate' was added so as not to interfere with the autonomy of the ex-officio organizations.
- § No objections from the committee to reinstating 'as a delegate' to Article 6, section 1, C.1.
- o S. Gile asks if terms for Executive Committee should be staggered to avoid the whole committee rolling over at the same time.

## Delegate &amp; Public Comments

- A. Houge notes Iowa passed legislation changing the title 'physician assistant' to 'physician associate' and asks if the committee has accounted for title changes in rulemaking?
- o Chair Loucka confirms the compact model language accounts for that in definitions.
- o L. Monick shares the compact definition for PA, ''PA' means an individual who is licensed as a physician assistant in a State. For purposes of this Compact, any other title or status adopted by a State to replace the term 'physician assistant' shall be deemed synonymous with 'physician assistant' and shall confer the same rights and responsibilities to the Licensee under the provisions of this Compact at the time of its enactment.'
