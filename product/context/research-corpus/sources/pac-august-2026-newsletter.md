## Chair Corner

It sometimes feels like I am a broken record, but the PA Compact just keeps growing.  Two more jurisdictions have joined since the last newsletter, and we are up to 29 members. This number should stay steady for a while as the majority of legislative sessions have wrapped up for the year.

PA Compact Commission committees and their members have remained busy, and we look forward to sharing that work at the Full Commission meeting on September 18.  The work of those committees keeps the PA Compact moving forward and preparing it for when the data system is created.

Though the start of data system development has been delayed, the PA Compact staff and Executive Committee have worked closely with both the Commission's funding partners and the vendor, and we hope to start work on the database by the end of August.  If that happens, the PA Compact plans to have a viable database in early 2027, which is in line with our previous projections.

Similar to other compact data system projects, the initial database project is just the start. There will need to be future contracts with vendors to maintain and improve the database and onboard states.  This will also require time and funds and, until states are onboarded and the system is being used regularly, the PA Compact remains dependent on the support of outside entities.  The Commission appreciates this support as it prevents assessing a fee on Member States.  If you are aware of funding opportunities that may help us reach that goal more quickly, please contact us at pacompact@csg.org.

As we continue to move forward, I would like to take a moment to look back and thank one of the original commission members, Larry Marx, from the state of Utah.  Larry was present at the original PA Compact Commission meeting and was elected to serve as Treasurer and Chair of the Finance Committee.  Larry has provided two annual budgets and worked to ensure that the funds currently available to the PA Compact are used wisely.  In addition, Larry set the groundwork for the process of determining what administrative fees are charged to PAs.  This is a complicated process, with many variables, and Larry has proceeded with thoughtful deliberation and concern for both the PA Compact and the PAs who will use the PA Compact.

<!-- image -->

Larry is beginning a new chapter, and I want to offer him my personal thanks as well as the appreciation and good wishes from the PA Compact Executive Committee. Thank you for all your contributions.  We wish you well.

- -Tim Terranova, PA Compact Commission Chair

## State Readiness -Preparing for Compact Activation

In anticipation of compact activation, Member States must ensure compliance with all compact requirements. The PA Compact Commission's State Preparation Guidelines cover administrative tasks Member States should consider prior to the launch of the Commission's data system.

Implementation of an FBI Criminal Background Check for PA licensure and setting a state privilege fee are two tasks of particular importance to a state's participation in the compact that may require extended time to complete.

## Criminal Background Check

The PA Compact requires that those who obtain privileges must have an FBI criminal background check (CBC), which state administrators must review when making a compact eligibility decision. Member States must fully implement an FBI CBC requirement pursuant to the Compact, which may require legislative enactment. The results of an FBI CBC can never be reported to the Commission. The qualifying state that is reviewing the FBI CBC alone determines compact eligibility of the PA.

## Setting a State Privilege Fee

Prior to issuing privileges, Member States must determine their state privilege fee. Member States should consider setting a fee that, when added to the commission fee, does not exceed the current single state licensure fee.

Setting a privilege fee may require licensing boards to promulgate a rule or specify the fee in their state's statute. Once the fee has been set, Member States should notify the Commission of the fee amount. Member States should also consider if militaryaffiliated practitioners will have a reduced fee.

## FAQ Highlight

## Are PAs with misdemeanor or felony convictions eligible to utilize the compact?

No. A PA who has a misdemeanor or felony conviction or pled guilty or no contest to a misdemeanor or felony charge is ineligible for participation. However, practitioners may

<!-- image -->

contact the licensing authority for the state wherein they wish to practice for alternate pathways to licensure, such as a single-state license.
