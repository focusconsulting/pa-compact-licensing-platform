## Domain Migration Update

## AAPA PA Compact Article -'Understanding the PA Compact'

- Chair Terranova notes the article was shared for the committee's awareness. The article contains good information, though some points were inaccurate like stating the commission issues licenses. The commission will continue to be the main source of information regarding the compact and ensure the information is accurate. Last week a reporter inquired about the PA Compact and was referred by AAPA to the PA Compact Commission, which was good progress in that regard. This article will be shared with the Communications Committee for their next meeting so they may use the article to formulate and response to possible FAQs.
- o E. Huntley asks if in their capacity as commissioners they use ' physician assistant ' or ' physician associate. '
- o Chair Terranova notes the commission has tried to avoid this confusion by relying on 'PA Compact' and 'PAs.'
- o J. Alley provides in the meeting chat the model legislation definition for PA:
- ''PA' means an individual who is licensed as a physician assistant in a State. For purposes of this Compact, any other title or status adopted by a State to replace the term 'physician assistant' shall be deemed synonymous with 'physician assistant' and shall confer the same rights and responsibilities to the Licensee under the provisions of this Compact at the time of its enactment.'
- o K. Scarbalis notes that the title change comes as a legislative adaptation that each state must make. States may not have lobbying resources to make the change, particularly if they are currently pursuing compact legislation.
- o G. Thomas explains that NCCPA has not changed their organization name to reflect 'physician associate' as AAPA has because the change has not yet occurred widely enough throughout the profession.
- o K. Scarbalis notes the content issue in the article regarding the commission issuing licenses will be communicated to the authors.
