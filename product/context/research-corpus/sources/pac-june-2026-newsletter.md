## Chair Corner

The PA Compact continues to grow.  By the end of the legislative session, we expect to have at least 27 states as members.  The adoption by states in just a few years is phenomenal and a testament to the desire of the PA profession to have a process that streamlines interstate practice.

The PA Compact is ironing out the last few contract details with our chosen data system vendor and hope to have the work underway shortly.  We continue to move forward with the goal of onboarding states and issuing privileges in early 2027.  In order to meet that goal, we ask that you continue to prepare for a fully functioning PA Compact.  If your state is already a member, this newsletter contains information regarding what you can do now. If your state is not a member, work with your licensing board, state association, and legislature to introduce the legislation needed to join. The PA Compact has a Toolkit page that provides resources.  Together we can make this project a success.

As we continue to move forward, I would like to take a moment to look back and thank one of the original commission members.  Kathleen ('Kathy') Scarbalis, PA -C, served as an exofficio member, representing the American Academy of Physician Associates (AAPA), from the beginning.  Kathy attended every meeting, whether full commission or smaller committee and represented the voice of the PA passionately and eloquently.  Her knowledge and experience as a PA informed non-PA members and directly impacted the first rules adopted by the PA Compact.  Kathy has stepped away from her role as the AAPA representative, and I want to offer Kathy my personal thanks as well as the appreciation and good wishes from the PA Compact Executive Committee.  Thank you for all your contributions, and we wish you well in your future endeavors.

- -Tim Terranova, PA Compact Commission Chair

## Meeting Synopsis

## Full Commission

The Full Commission met April 6, 2026, to review and adopt Rule 3 -Compact Privilege and Rule 4 -Compact Data System, Confidentiality, and Information Sharing. Both rules were adopted at the public meeting with an effective date of May 31, 2026.

## Executive Committee

On May 13, 2026, the Executive Committee met in closed session to discuss the Commission's funding Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) with the American Academy of Physician Associates and the National Commission on Certification of Physician Assistants. Upon leaving closed session, the committee voted to move draft rule 7 on state compliance to public comment. The committee reviewed draft bylaw amendments from the Rules Committee that addressed commission officer vacancies and tabled moving the amendments to public comment pending the receipt of further bylaw amendments to be provided by the Rules Committee after its June meeting. The Executive Committee also voted to delegate Product Manager responsibilities for the Commission's data system to Abigail Mortell, who will work in consultation with Commission Chair, and the Technology Subcommittee when possible.

On April 8, 2026, the Executive Committee convened and voted to delegate responsibility of overseeing data system development to the Technology Subcommittee. The subcommittee shall provide monthly status reports to the Executive Committee. The committee also voted to move draft rule 5 on joint investigations to public comment.

During the March 11, 2026, meeting of the Executive Committee, the committee voted to renew the Commission's contract with The Council of State Governments (CSG) for secretariat and legal services. Chair Terranova expressed the Commission's intent to convene a Technology Subcommittee to oversee data system development and requested nominations for volunteers to serve on the committee. These volunteers will be commissioners, alternates, and members of licensing board staff and IT departments. The subcommittee will also assist the data system vendor with user story research.

## State Readiness -Preparing for Compact Activation

In anticipation of compact activation, Member States must ensure compliance with all compact requirements. The PA Compact Commission's State Preparation Guidelines

<!-- image -->

cover administrative tasks Member States should consider prior to the launch of the Commission's data system.

Implementation of an FBI Criminal Background Check for PA licensure and setting a state privilege fee are two tasks of particular importance to a state's participation in the Compact that may require extended time to complete.

## Criminal Background Check

The PA Compact requires that those who obtain privileges must have an FBI criminal background check (CBC), which state administrators must review when making a compact eligibility decision. Member States must fully implement an FBI Criminal Background Check requirement pursuant to the Compact, which may require legislative enactment. The results of a CBC can never be reported to the Commission. The qualifying state that is reviewing the FBI CBC alone determines compact eligibility of the PA.

## Setting a State Privilege Fee

Prior to issuing privileges, Member States must determine their state privilege fee. Member States should consider setting a fee that, when added to the commission fee, does not exceed the current single state licensure fee.

Setting a privilege fee may require licensing boards to promulgate a rule or specify the fee in their state's statute. Once the fee has been set, Member States should notify the Commission of the fee amount. Member States should also consider if military-affiliated practitioners will have a reduced fee.
